David — I’m building OmaStore as an independent experiment in what a storefront could add to Omarchy.

It’s a native Qt application with a Rust core. The twist is selective setups: see a useful workflow, inspect its apps and settings, then take the pieces you want. Keep the source, cost, privileges and actual test evidence visible before committing to a change. Save your selection as an attributed remix someone else can build on.

The running playground covers discovery, maker pages, submissions, independent review, installation recovery and author income. Listings and ordinary updates are free; authors keep their own checkout and support links with no OmaStore fee. Optional managed checkout has a working test lifecycle, with real payments still gated.

The demo uses fictional listings. Real Omarchy validation and genuine publisher participation are the next proof points.

Where would you draw the line between a storefront and Omarchy itself?
