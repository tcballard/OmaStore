David — I've been building OmaStore to explore what an application storefront could feel like on Omarchy.

It's a native Qt application with a Rust core. The running build has app discovery, maker pages, private submission and review workflows, and release feeds. It's still a development build: the public shelves are empty, and the local publication rehearsal is explicitly simulated.

The part I want to push further is selective setups. Show someone a useful workflow, let them inspect every component, then take the pieces they want. Keep source, price, privileges and actual Omarchy test evidence visible before anything touches their machine.

Submissions would be free. Makers could sell or receive support through their own checkout without an OmaStore fee.

I'm building it independently to find out what works. Where would you draw the line between a storefront and Omarchy itself?
